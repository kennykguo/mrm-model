    The 8 rings share one bus waveguide. Choose ring color to compare temperature, lock error or detuning; heater colors show heater temperature.     • Drawn wider: the waveguides (rib 0.4 µm) are drawn 3× wide so they show at full view.

    Why is the ring bistable?

    This separate demonstration exports only ring 0, swept open loop; it is not an eight-ring comparison. The ring's own light heats it and pulls its resonance red. Between the two folds one heater code holds two stable temperatures; the middle branch cannot be held open loop. Not tied to the scrubber.

    Who heats whom? The static coupling

    Each cell answers: how much would this observer (row) warm if this source (column) supplied 1 mW long enough to settle? Stronger color means stronger coupling on a logarithmic scale. Off-diagonal cells show heat spreading to other devices; these are static gains, not the current temperatures.

    Observers: each ring (mode-weighted), its heater resistor, its monitor PD, and the die sensor. Sources: each ring's heater, absorbed light, junction and driver, and the two aggressor sites. On the rings × heaters view the diagonal is the heater efficiency.

    How fast does the heat arrive?

    One trace per ring: the thermal response to a 1 mW step in its own heater. Faster rises mean quicker heat paths; the plateau is the final coupling. Identical model paths overlap; the selected ring is thicker.

    Which sources are heating each ring at this instant?

    Rows are heated rings; columns identify the ring or site supplying heat. Cell values are temperature rises in K at the nearest key frame, with stronger color for a larger contribution. The outlined row is the selected ring; source buttons choose which heat paths to include.
