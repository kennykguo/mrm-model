\section*{How to use this guide}

You know textbook chapters 0 to 5. This guide starts from that knowledge. It explains how the model works and shows
where each part is in the code.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Section~\ref{sec:five} gives five ideas that you need before you read any code. Read it first.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:words} defines the words that the code and this guide use.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:one} shows the whole model on one page.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:map} lists the core code in reading order.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:bridge} explains each physics part, from your Ch05 ring to the full model.
  \item Section~\ref{sec:next} lists what remains to learn and the first commands to run.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Equation numbers.} ``\eq{5.26}'' means equation 5.26 in textbook chapter 5. This guide always writes the
equation out. ``Not in the textbook'' marks an equation that the textbook does not have.

\textbf{Numbers.} Every model number comes from the preset \texttt{g26}: the file \cd{mrm/params/presets/g26.yaml},
or the command \texttt{mrm params explain g26 <key>}. The textbook's own numbers are not verified, so do not use them
as design values. A row ``Book'' tells you where the textbook uses another value or another method. ``My arithmetic''
marks a number that I calculated from model values. No model output gives it directly.

\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{\textbf{Sign convention.} The model detuning is
$d=\lambda_{res}-\lambda_{line}$. Ch05 uses $\delta=\lambda_L-\lambda_r$, so $d=-\delta$. A positive $d$ means that
the resonance is red of (at a longer wavelength than) its laser line. The laser is then on the blue flank of the
dip. This is the stable lock side. Two sign conventions are in use, so always say where the resonance is relative to
the laser. The code writes $i$ for your $j$. Time dependence is $e^{+i\omega t}$, and one round trip multiplies a
field by $a\,e^{-i\phi}$. This is your Ch05 convention.}}

\section{Five ideas you need first}\label{sec:five}

The numbers here are for ring 0 of g26, cold and at its own resonance. The model gives these inputs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item loaded $Q_L=3850$ (the Q of the whole ring, with every loss, \texttt{ring.optics.q\_loaded}), FSR $=1.8$\,THz (\texttt{ring.optics.fsr\_thz}),
  resonance wavelength $\lambda=1311.04$\,nm, so $f=c/\lambda=228.67$\,THz;
  \item power coupling per pass $\kappa_1^2=0.09402$ (through) and $\kappa_2^2=0.00145$ (drop), round-trip amplitude
  $a=0.94784$;
  \item so $t_1=\sqrt{1-0.09402}=0.9518$, $t_2=\sqrt{1-0.00145}=0.99927$, and $t_1t_2a=0.9518\times0.99927\times0.94784=0.9015$.
\end{itemize}
The product $t_1t_2a$ is the fraction of the field that one lap keeps. For the add-drop ring it takes the place of
the $ta$ of the all-pass ring.

\subsection{The comb of dips: FSR, linewidth, finesse and Q}\label{sec:comb}

Plot the through power against wavelength (Figure~\ref{fig:comb}). You get a comb of narrow dips. Four numbers
describe the comb.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Where the dips are.} A dip occurs when a whole number $m$ of wavelengths fits around the ring:
  $\phi=2\pi m$ \eq{5.9}.
  \item \textbf{FSR} (free spectral range): the spacing between two dips. $\Delta f_{FSR}=c/(n_gL)$ \eq{5.14} and
  $\Delta\lambda_{FSR}=\lambda^2/(n_gL)$ \eq{5.13}. Model: $\Delta\lambda_{FSR}=\lambda^2\Delta f_{FSR}/c
  =(1311.04\,\mathrm{nm})^2\times1.8\,\mathrm{THz}/c=10.32$\,nm.
  \item \textbf{Linewidth} (FWHM): the width of one dip at half depth.
  $\Delta\lambda_{FWHM}=(1-ta)\lambda^2/(\pi n_gL\sqrt{ta})$ \eq{5.21}. In frequency this is
  $\Delta f_{FWHM}=(1-ta)\Delta f_{FSR}/(\pi\sqrt{ta})=0.0985\times1800\,\mathrm{GHz}/(\pi\times0.9495)=59.4$\,GHz,
  which is 0.341\,nm. The model's exact value is 59.39\,GHz, 340.5\,pm (\texttt{fwhm\_pm}).
  \item \textbf{Finesse} $\mathcal{F}$: the FSR divided by the linewidth, $\mathcal{F}=\pi\sqrt{ta}/(1-ta)$ \eq{5.25}.
  It says how sharp the dips are compared with their spacing. It depends only on $ta$, the field kept per lap.
  Model: $\mathcal{F}=1800\,\mathrm{GHz}/59.39\,\mathrm{GHz}=30.3$.
  \item \textbf{Q} (quality factor): the light's own frequency divided by the linewidth,
  $Q=\lambda/\Delta\lambda_{FWHM}=f/\Delta f_{FWHM}$ \eq{5.22}. Model: $Q=228.67\,\mathrm{THz}/59.39\,\mathrm{GHz}=3850$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{How they are linked.} Divide \eq{5.22} by \eq{5.25}: $Q=\mathcal{F}\times f/\Delta f_{FSR}$. Model:
$30.3\times(228.67/1.8)=30.3\times127.0=3850$. Equation 5.23 gives the same link with $\sqrt{ta}$ set to 1.

\subsection{Photon lifetime and steady state}\label{sec:life}

\textbf{The RC picture.} Apply a voltage step $V_s$ to an RC circuit. The capacitor voltage is
$v(t)=V_s\,(1-e^{-t/RC})$. It settles to a constant value. After $4.6\,RC$ it is within 1\,\% of $V_s$, because
$e^{-4.6}=0.01$. ``Steady state'' means this settled condition: nothing changes any more.

\textbf{The ring stores energy like a capacitor.} Let $U$ be the optical energy in the ring. Ch05 (section ``Quality
factor, finesse, lifetime, build-up'', part ``Finesse, photon lifetime, build-up'') derives the free decay. Start
from $Q=\omega U/P$ \eq{5.22}, with lost power $P=-dU/dt$:
\[
\frac{dU}{dt}=-\frac{\omega U}{Q}\ \Rightarrow\ U(t)=U(0)\,e^{-t/\tau_p},\qquad
\tau_p=\frac{Q}{\omega}=\frac{Q\lambda}{2\pi c}\ \text{\eq{5.26}}.
\]
$\tau_p$ is the \emph{photon lifetime}. Ch00 calls it the ring's ringdown time. Model:
$\tau_p=3850\times1.311\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}/(2\pi\times2.998\times10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s})=2.68$\,ps.

\textbf{Check from the laps.} One lap takes $T_{rt}=1/\Delta f_{FSR}=1/1.8\,\mathrm{THz}=0.556$\,ps. Per lap the
field keeps $t_1t_2a=0.9015$, so the power keeps $0.9015^2=0.8128$. After $n$ laps $U_n=U_0\,(0.8128)^n$. So
$\tau_p=T_{rt}/(-\ln0.8128)=0.556\,\mathrm{ps}/0.2073=2.68$\,ps. This agrees. In laps, the lifetime is
$\tau_p/T_{rt}=\mathcal{F}/2\pi=30.3/6.28=4.8$ laps.

\textbf{The build-up after the input turns on.} The field decays twice as slowly as the energy, with time constant
$2\tau_p$ (Ch05 states this). The lap sum \eq{5.6} shows the build-up. After $n$ laps, the field misses the terms of
lap $n$ and later. Their sum is exactly $(t_1t_2a)^n$ times the final field. So the missing part shrinks by 0.9015
per lap. That is the RC step response, with $RC=2\tau_p=5.4$\,ps (Figure~\ref{fig:build}). The field is within 1\,\%
of its final value after $\ln100/(-\ln0.9015)=44$ laps, which is $44\times0.556=24.7$\,ps.

\textbf{Steady state of the ring.} After that time the stored energy is constant, so the through and drop powers are
constant. Equation 5.30 gives the powers in this state only:
\[
T_{through}=\frac{t_2^2a^2-2t_1t_2a\cos\phi+t_1^2}{1-2t_1t_2a\cos\phi+(t_1t_2a)^2},\qquad
T_{drop}=\frac{(1-t_1^2)(1-t_2^2)\,a}{1-2t_1t_2a\cos\phi+(t_1t_2a)^2}\quad\text{\eq{5.30}}.
\]

\subsection{Why the model can use the steady-state formulas}\label{sec:why}

The model is a program that moves time forward in fixed steps. One step is a \emph{tick}. One tick is 1\us{}
(\texttt{sim.dt\_plant\_s}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item One tick holds $1\us/0.556\,\mathrm{ps}=1.8$ million laps.
  \item The ring settles in about 44 laps (24.7\,ps). So at every tick the ring is long settled.
  \item So the program does not add laps one at a time. It does not simulate the light as it travels. Once per ring
  and per tick, it puts the present $\phi$ into \eq{5.30}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{What changes between ticks is the temperature.} The silicon index rises with temperature. In the model the
effective index rises by $1.88\times10^{-4}$ per kelvin at 25\degC{} (\texttt{ring.optics.dn\_eff\_dt\_per\_k}). So $\phi$ rises and the
resonance moves red. Your shift formula $\Delta\lambda_r=(\lambda/n_g)\,\Delta n_{eff}$ \eq{5.32} gives the slope.
With the small thermal expansion of the ring added, the model has 65.1\,pm/K at 25\degC{} and 73.9\,pm/K at the lock
temperature (134\degC). Ch09 covers this effect. Heat changes slowly, like the voltage of an RC circuit. The thermal
time constants of the model go from 0.4\us{} to several milliseconds.

So each tick does three things:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Update every temperature, like one RC step over 1\us.
  \item Compute $\phi$ from the new temperature.
  \item Put $\phi$ into \eq{5.30}.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{EE picture.} The optics is a resistor divider: its output follows its input at once, and it has no memory.
The heat is a capacitor: it keeps its value from one tick to the next.

\subsection{Self-heating and the Newton solve}\label{sec:newton}

\textbf{Self-heating.} The ring loses part of its light. This is the loss $a$. By energy conservation the lost
power is $P_{in}(1-T_{through}-T_{drop})$, with both powers from \eq{5.30}. Most of it is absorbed and becomes heat:
\[
P_{abs}=\eta\,P_{in}\,(1-T_{through}-T_{drop}),\qquad \eta=0.848\ \text{(the absorbed share of the loss)}.
\]
The heat raises the ring temperature, like a current through a resistor raises a voltage. Over one tick the
model uses
\[
T=T_{other}+g\,P_{abs},\qquad g=\sum_j\big(1-e^{-h/\tau_j}\big)\,r_j\quad\text{\nib}.
\]
Here $h=1\us$ is the tick. $T_{other}$ is the temperature that the ring would have at the end of the tick with no
absorbed light in this tick. Each pole $j$ is one RC section of the heat path, with DC gain $r_j$ and time constant
$\tau_j$. The factor $1-e^{-h/\tau_j}$ is how far that RC section charges in one tick. Model (ring 0, light to ring):
$g=4.34(1-e^{-1/0.4})+2.31(1-e^{-1/3.18})+3.10(1-e^{-1/13.0})+0.29(1-e^{-1/3000})=3.98+0.62+0.23+0.00=4.83$\,K/mW.
The DC value (the sum of all $r_j$) is 10.0\,K/mW. This guide calls this whole step ``the heat model over one tick''.

\textbf{Why a few kelvin matter.} One linewidth is $340.5\,\mathrm{pm}/73.9\,\mathrm{pm/K}=4.6$\,K. At the lock
each ring absorbs about 1.5\,mW (the model's lock solve \texttt{solve\_locked\_steady\_state}, 25\degC, 1.05\,V bias). At DC that gives
$10.0\,\mathrm{K/mW}\times1.5\,\mathrm{mW}=15$\,K, about 1.1\,nm of resonance shift.

\textbf{The loop.} Temperature sets $\phi$. $\phi$ sets $P_{abs}$. $P_{abs}$ sets the temperature. The optics has no
delay inside a tick. So the end-of-tick temperature must satisfy one equation per ring:
\[
f(T)=T-T_{other}-g\,P_{abs}\big(\phi(T)\big)=0.
\]
The function has a cosine inside a fraction, so there is no algebraic solution. The program uses \emph{Newton's
method} (Figure~\ref{fig:newton}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Start from the previous tick's temperature $T_0$.
  \item Draw the tangent line of $f$ at $T_0$. Its zero crossing is the next guess: $T_1=T_0-f(T_0)/f'(T_0)$.
  \item Repeat until $|f(T)|<10^{-7}$\,K (\texttt{DEFAULT\_SH\_TOL\_K} in \cd{mrm/plant/plant.py}). The code allows at
  most 10 Newton steps. With the evaluation at $T_0$, that is at most 11 evaluations.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{How accurate and how fast.} Near the root, each step roughly squares the error. In a steady g26 run I
measured 1 to 2 Newton steps per ring and tick, mean 1.8 (my measurement, 3000 ticks).

\textbf{Near a fold.} With strong self-heating, one heater power can give two stable ring temperatures. Ch10 calls
this \emph{bistability}. Each stable solution is a \emph{branch}. A branch ends at a \emph{fold}, where $f'(T)\to0$.
There a plain Newton step can jump to the wrong root. So the code shortens each step to a distance that cannot
contain a root. It always follows the root that the ring was already on (section~\ref{sec:bist}).

\subsection{What one tick does}\label{sec:tick}

First, four parts of the hardware:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Monitor photodiode (PD):} a germanium photodiode on each ring's drop port. Its current is
  $0.95\,\mathrm{A/W}\times$ the drop power.
  \item \textbf{ADC} (analog-to-digital converter): turns the PD current into an integer, the \emph{ADC code}.
  It has 16 bits (codes 0 to 65\,535). One code is $40\uA/65\,535=0.610$\,nA.
  \item \textbf{Heater:} a resistor inside each ring. Its power heats the ring and moves the resonance red.
  \item \textbf{DAC} (digital-to-analog converter): turns an integer, the \emph{heater code}, into a voltage. It has
  16 bits. Code 0 gives 0\,V and code 65\,535 gives 1.6\,V.
\end{itemize}
At each tick (1\us), for every ring:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item At every 10th tick (100\,kHz), your controller runs. It reads the ADC codes that arrived since its last
  run. It writes new heater codes. A code reaches the DAC after a delay (latency) of 1\us.
  \item The scenario sets the outside inputs: ambient temperature, disturbance heat, laser powers and frequencies,
  data on or off.
  \item The DAC turns the heater code into a voltage. The heater power is $P=V^2/R$.
  \item The phase model computes $\phi$ at each laser line. It uses the ring temperature and the data voltage on
  the ring's PN junction (section~\ref{sec:mod}).
  \item The heat model over one tick moves every temperature forward. For each ring, in the order that the light
  meets them, the Newton solve of section~\ref{sec:newton} finds the end-of-tick temperature. The light that a ring
  passes goes on to the next ring.
  \item The monitor PD collects the drop current. Every 4 ticks the ADC makes one code. The code reaches the
  controller 2\us{} later.
  \item The program records the true state, for example the true detuning. Your controller never sees it.
\end{enumerate}
The loop closes like this: heater code $\to$ heater power $\to$ ring temperature $\to$ $\phi$ $\to$ drop power
$\to$ ADC code $\to$ your controller $\to$ heater code.

variation, LHS & Variation is the ring-to-ring spread inside one world. LHS (Latin hypercube sampling) spreads $N$ random worlds evenly over every range.\\
Truth, oracle & Truth is the true state (for example the true detuning). The program knows it, your controller does not. The oracle is a benchmark that reads Truth and finds the best stable operating point. You may not use it as your controller.\\
warm start, cold start & A warm start begins with every ring near its lock. A cold start begins with the heaters off.\\
start protocol & How the first tick picks the self-heating branch: \texttt{branch\_low}, \texttt{branch\_high} or \texttt{laser\_ramp}.\\
diagnostic & A check number that the model computes from the preset, for example \texttt{fwhm\_pm}.\\
excursion & A time interval in which a ring is out of lock.\\
harness, telemetry & A harness is a simpler test setup outside the engine. Telemetry is the debug output of a controller.\\
test and tag names & A1--A4, B3, B5: Lightmatter's acceptance tests (Ch18). A1 is the aggressor test. CAL-$n$, VAL-$n$, BND-$n$: the model's calibration, validation and bound checks. ``07 P0-1'', ``07 D9'', ``Q4'': items in \cd{docs/research/07_design_critique.md}.\\
knowledge tokens & Names in the preset that say which design numbers your controller may read.\\
latch, FIFO & A latch is a register that holds a command until the hardware takes it. A FIFO (first in, first out) is a queue. Here it is a delay line for a command.\\
trace, metrics & A trace is a recorded time signal. Metrics are the summary numbers of a run.\\
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{The device and the physics}}\\
G25, G26, imec & G25 and G26 are generations of Lightmatter's device. imec is a research institute. The model borrows its thermal data.\\
WDM, line & Wavelength-division multiplexing: 8 laser lines share one waveguide, the \emph{bus}. Each ring modulates its own line.\\
foreign line, wrong line & A foreign line is the line of another ring. A wrong-line ring has locked onto a foreign line.\\
bus order & The order in which the light meets the rings (ring 0 first).\\
NRZ & Non-return-to-zero data. Each bit holds one of two levels for one bit time.\\
PN junction & A diode across the ring waveguide. Reverse bias removes free carriers, so $n_{eff}$ rises and the loss falls (Ch06).\\
OMA, relOMA & OMA (optical modulation amplitude) is the difference between the output powers for a ``1'' and a ``0''. relOMA is the OMA divided by the ring's input power.\\
extinction ratio & The ratio of the ``1'' and ``0'' output powers.\\
BER & Bit error ratio: the fraction of received bits that are wrong.\\
side-of-fringe lock & A lock with the laser on a flank of the dip, not at its bottom. There a small shift gives the largest change of power.\\
thermo-optic effect & The index of silicon rises with temperature (Ch09).\\
self-heating, branch, fold, bistability & See section~\ref{sec:newton} and Ch10.\\
self-heating relief & The factor by which self-heating divides the heater's effect on the red side (section~\ref{sec:sh}).\\
heater authority & The range of heat that the heater can supply. It can only add heat, never remove it.\\
TCR & Temperature coefficient of resistance: the fractional change of the heater resistance per kelvin.\\
supply limit & All heaters share one supply. Above its power or current limit, every heater gets less power.\\
thermal pole & One first-order lag of a heat path, like one RC section: a DC gain $r$ (K/W) and a time constant $\tau$.\\
Foster sum & A heat path written as a sum of poles, $\sum_k r_k/(1+s\tau_k)$: parallel RC sections in series.\\
ZOH, FOH & Zero-order hold: the input is constant across a tick. First-order hold: the input changes linearly across a tick.\\
explicit, implicit & An explicit update uses the previous tick's value. An implicit update solves for the end-of-tick value (section~\ref{sec:newton}).\\
crosstalk & Heat from one heater or ring that reaches another ring.\\
aggressor & An on-die heater next to ring 0. The test drives it as a disturbance (scenario A1).\\
dark current & The current of a photodiode with no light. It grows with temperature.\\
TCMT & Temporal coupled-mode theory. The steady-state comparison is archived. Waveform analysis uses its time-domain form.\\

\subsection{The model ring is your add-drop ring}\label{sec:ring}
The exact field calculation includes clockwise and counterclockwise light. With backscatter set to zero,
it reduces to your add-drop equations \eq{5.30}, without the Lorentzian approximation. For each ring and
each of the 8 lines, \cd{ring_rows_nb} computes power fractions and stored energies per watt of input.
The zero-backscatter through power $T$, drop power $D$, and loss fraction $\mathcal{L}$ are:
\begin{gather*}
t_j=\sqrt{1-K_j^2},\qquad k_j=\sqrt{K_j^2},\qquad a=e^{-\alpha_i L/2},\qquad
\mathcal{D}(\phi)=1-2t_1t_2a\cos\phi+(t_1t_2a)^2,\\
T(\phi)=\frac{t_1^2-2t_1t_2a\cos\phi+t_2^2a^2}{\mathcal{D}(\phi)},\qquad D(\phi)=\frac{k_1^2k_2^2\,a}{\mathcal{D}(\phi)},\qquad
\mathcal{L}(\phi)=\frac{k_1^2}{\mathcal{D}(\phi)}\,(1-a)\,(1+t_2^2a),\qquad P_{abs}=\eta\,\mathcal{L}\,P_{in}.
\end{gather*}
$T$ and $D$ are \eq{5.30}. The cosine makes each formula periodic in $\phi$, so one evaluation covers every
resonance order. The loss term $\mathcal{L}$ is not in the textbook. It has three factors:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $k_1^2/\mathcal{D}$ is the power that circulates just after the input coupler. At resonance it is your
  build-up factor $\kappa^2/(1-ta)^2$ \eq{5.27}, with $ta$ replaced by $t_1t_2a$.
  \item $1-a$ is the power fraction lost in one half of the ring.
  \item $1+t_2^2a$ adds the second half: the power $t_2^2a$ continues past the drop coupler.
\end{itemize}
Only the fraction $\eta$ of that loss is absorption that heats the ring. The rest is scattering.

The preset does not give $t_1$, $t_2$ and $a$. It gives the loaded Q, the drop share $\kappa_d/\kappa$ and a loss
budget. \cd{relations.py} (R4) converts them with the exact energy decay over one round trip $T_{rt}$
\nib:
\[
K_j^2=1-\exp(-\omega T_{rt}/Q_j),\quad a=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\omega T_{rt}}{2Q_i}\right),\quad
\frac1{Q_L}=\frac1{Q_i}+\frac1{Q_t}+\frac1{Q_d},\quad Q_d=\frac{Q_L}{\kappa_d/\kappa}.
\]
The sum of reciprocals is your $1/Q\approx1/Q_{int}+1/Q_c$ \eq{5.24}, with a third channel for the drop coupler.

\subsection{Where the resonance is: the phase model}
Your shift formula $\Delta\lambda_r=(\lambda/n_g)\Delta n_{eff}$ \eq{5.32} moves a resonance. The model computes this
shift once per ring and tick, from the ring temperature $T$ and the junction voltage $V$ (\cd{phase.py} equation 1,
not in the textbook):
\[
\Delta\lambda(T,V)=s_0\Big[(T-T_{ref})+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big((T-T_0)^2-(T_{ref}-T_0)^2\big)\Big]+\frac{\lambda_{res}}{n_g}\Big[f\,\Delta n_{eff}(V)-f\,\Delta n_{eff}(V_{ref})\Big].
\]
The first term is the heat (section~\ref{sec:to}). The second term is the junction (section~\ref{sec:mod}). The model
then moves the whole cold phase curve by that shift. This is a ``rigid comb'': every order moves by the same amount.
It reads the phase at each laser line (equation 2, not in the textbook):
\begin{gather*}
x=\lambda_l-\Delta\lambda,\qquad \Delta=x-\lambda_{res},\qquad
n(x)=n_{eff}+(n_{eff}-n_g)\frac{\Delta}{\lambda_{res}}-\frac{n_g'\,\Delta^2}{2\lambda_{res}},\qquad \phi=\frac{2\pi\,n(x)\,L}{x}.
\end{gather*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\phi=2\pi m$ \eq{5.9} exactly when the shifted resonance reaches the line.
  \item Each ring is built 7.6\,nm blue of its own line: $\lambda_{res}^{cold}=\lambda_{line}-\Delta\lambda_{off}$.
  The heater must close this gap.
  \item The order is $m=\mathrm{round}(n_{eff}L/\lambda_{res})$. R3 then sets $n_{eff}=m\lambda_{res}/L$, so the cold
  resonance sits exactly at the offset.
  \item The couplings are taken at the line: $K_j^2(\lambda_l)=K_{j,0}^2\exp[\partial_\lambda\ln K_j^2\,(\lambda_l-\lambda_{res})]$.
  So under lock the dip depth does not change with the heater code.
  \item The loss follows the junction:
  $\alpha_i=[\alpha_{i,ref}+f\alpha_{fca}(V)-f\alpha_{fca}(V_{ref})]\,e^{c_R(T-T_{ref})+c_H(T_H-T_{ref})}$, and
  $\eta=\alpha_{heat}/\alpha_i$ (equation 5). In g26 $c_R=c_H=0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modulation: the PN junction and the two data levels}\label{sec:mod}
Free carriers lower the silicon index and absorb light (the plasma-dispersion effect, Ch06). A PN junction runs
around a fraction $f$ of the ring. Reverse bias widens the depletion region (the zone with no free carriers). So
$n_{eff}$ rises, the resonance moves red, and the loss falls. In an abrupt junction the depletion width grows as
$\sqrt{V+V_{bi}}$, with $V_{bi}$ the built-in voltage. So the model uses a square-root law (not in the textbook). Here
$m=0.5$ is the depletion exponent, not the resonance order:
\begin{gather*}
\Delta\lambda_J(V)=A\big[(V+V_{bi})^{m}-V_{bi}^{m}\big],\qquad A=\frac{\mathrm{eff}_0\,V_{bi}^{1-m}}{m},\qquad
\frac{d\lambda}{dV}=A\,m\,(V+V_{bi})^{m-1},\\
\Delta n_{eff}(V)=\frac{n_g}{\lambda}\,\Delta\lambda_{full}(V),\qquad \alpha_{fca}(V)=\alpha_0-\frac{4\pi q}{\lambda}\,\Delta n_{eff}(V).
\end{gather*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathrm{eff}_0$ is the shift per volt at 0\,V. $\alpha_0$ is the free-carrier loss at 0\,V.
  \item $q=\lambda\Delta\alpha/(4\pi\Delta n)$ is the scaled ratio of loss change to index change.
  \item $\Delta\lambda_{full}=\Delta\lambda_J/f$ is the shift of a junction around the full ring. The code multiplies
  the index and loss change by $f$ again at run time.
  \item The data swings the junction between $V_0=V_b-V_{pp}/2$ and $V_1=V_b+V_{pp}/2$ (bias $V_b$, swing $V_{pp}$).
  Level 1 has the higher reverse voltage, so it is redder and has less loss.
  \item The ring is solved at both levels every tick. Every output is the average over the ones density $p_1$:
  $\bar X=p_1X(\phi_1,a_1,\eta_1)+(1-p_1)X(\phi_0,a_0,\eta_0)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{OMA and relOMA}

\begin{gather*}

\subsection{Self-heating: an inner feedback loop}\label{sec:sh}
Section~\ref{sec:newton} gave the idea. Here is the loop view (Ch10). The absorbed power peaks near resonance, like
the drop curve. Heat moves the resonance red, and that changes the absorbed power again. Treat this as an inner
proportional loop around the ring temperature (none of these equations is in the textbook):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $Z_0$ is the DC thermal resistance from the heater to its ring (K/mW).
  \item $z$ says that one milliwatt of absorbed light heats the ring $z$ times more than one milliwatt of heater power.
  \item The self-heating gain $G_{sh}$ converts absorbed power to resonance shift.
  \item $u$ is the detuning that the heater alone would give.
\end{itemize}
\[
G_{sh}=s(T)\,Z_{opt},\quad Z_{opt}=z\,Z_0;\qquad \text{stable if and only if } G_{sh}\,\frac{\partial P_{abs}}{\partial\lambda_{res}}<1;\qquad
\frac{dd}{du}=\frac{1}{1+G_{sh}\,|P_{abs}'|}\ \ (\text{red side}).
\]
On the red side ($d>0$), heat moves the resonance away from the laser. So $P_{abs}$ falls and opposes the heat. This
is negative feedback. The \emph{relief} $1+G_{sh}|P_{abs}'|$ divides the effect of the heater and of every other
disturbance. On the blue side the feedback is positive. The code solves the loop implicitly, with the Newton solve of
section~\ref{sec:newton}:
\[
F(T_k)=T_k-T_k^{other}-g_k\,P_{abs,k}(T_k)=0,\qquad g_k=\sum_j(1-a_j)\,r_j^{opt},\quad a_j=e^{-h/\tau_j}.
\]
An explicit update (with the previous tick's $P_{abs}$) oscillates with a period of 2 ticks at 1\us{} steps. So the
code does not use one. A start from the previous root keeps the ring on its own branch.

\subsection{The heater law}

rises with the heater temperature (TCR), and that makes the gain droop at high drive. The code solves this inside
the tick, with $T_H=t_{pre}+g_{self}P$. Here $t_{pre}$ is the heater temperature without this tick's power.
$g_{self}$ (K/W) gives the rise from this tick's own power (not in the textbook):
\begin{gather*}
u=\frac{\mathrm{code}}{2^{16}-1},\qquad V=u\,V_{FS},\qquad R=R_0\big[1+\alpha_{TCR}(T_H-T_0)\big],\\
P=\frac{2V^2}{r_o+\sqrt{r_o^2+4kV^2}},\qquad r_o=R_0\big[1+\alpha_{TCR}(t_{pre}-T_0)\big],\qquad k=R_0\,\alpha_{TCR}\,g_{self}.

$s=\min(1,\sqrt{P_{max}/\sum P},\,I_{max}/\sum I)$ and $P\to s^2P$. Here $s$ is the droop factor, not the
thermo-optic slope. The tuning efficiency is $\eta_H=\mathrm{FSR}_\lambda/P_{FSR}$.

\subsection{Thermal dynamics and crosstalk}
Heat flow obeys the same equations as an RC network. Power is the current, and temperature rise is the voltage. The
model writes every path from a heat source to an observer (a point whose temperature it tracks) as a Foster sum of
poles. Each pole is one RC lag. The code advances each lag exactly over a step $h$, for a held input (ZOH,
$u_0=u_1$) or a linear one (FOH). None of this is in the textbook:
\[
Z(s)=\sum_k\frac{r_k}{1+s\tau_k},\qquad
x_m\leftarrow a_mx_m+r_m\big(b_0u_0+b_1u_1\big),\quad a=e^{-h/\tau},\ b_1=1-\frac{1-a}{h/\tau},\ b_0=\frac{1-a}{h/\tau}-a.
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item The heater reaches its own ring through three poles:
  $Z_{RH}(s)=Z_0\big[\frac{f_F}{1+s\tau_F}+\frac{1-f_F-f_T}{1+s\tau_S}+\frac{f_T}{1+s\tau_T}\big]$.

  \item Ring-to-ring crosstalk is one pole per pair, $cZ_0/(1+s\tau_{xt}(d))$. It is slower for a larger distance $d$.
  \item PDs, the die temperature sensor and the aggressor use the same distance laws.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The monitor and the ADC: what a code means}
Each ring has a germanium PD on its drop port. The controller never sees the current. It sees 16-bit codes. The PD
current, plus a dark current that grows with the PD temperature, is integrated over a window $W$. One noise sample
with the exact variance is added, and the ADC converts the mean (not in the textbook):
\[
i_{sig}=g_s\,\mathcal{R}\,(P_{drop}+f_{bg}P_{bus}),\qquad
\bar x=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{k\in(s-W,s]}(i_{sig}+i_d)+\sigma \xi,\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{S}{2T_W},\qquad
\mathrm{LSB}=\frac{I_{FS}}{2^{16}-1}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item $g_s$ is the monitor gain scale. $\mathcal{R}$ is the responsivity (A/W). $f_{bg}$ is the background fraction
  (0 in g26).
  \item $W$ is the window in ticks ($T_W$ in seconds). $s$ is the sample tick.
  \item $S=2q(i_{sig}+i_d)+S_{TIA}$ is the one-sided noise density. $q$ is the electron charge. $S_{TIA}$ is the noise
  of the amplifier (TIA, transimpedance amplifier) after the PD.
  \item $\xi$ is one draw from a unit normal distribution. The LSB is the current of one code.
\end{itemize}
The drop reading peaks at the resonance and is even in detuning. On one known flank the level gives the direction. On
the red lock side, more heater moves the resonance further red, so the code falls ($dy/du<0$).

\hard{Noise is not the limit. Shot noise in one 2\us{} sample is
$\sqrt{2q\times15.5\uA/(2\times2\us)}=1.1$\,nA. The monitor slope at the lock is about $0.12\uA$/pm, so the noise is
about 0.01\,pm (my arithmetic). Systematic errors dominate: the monitor gain (worlds scale it by 0.5--2), the dark
current, and light from foreign lines. An extra current $\Delta i$ moves a level lock by $\Delta i$ divided by the
monitor slope. So the shipped locks normalize to a peak that they measure.}

\cd{monitor_tick_row} 337--467; \cd{pi_lock.py} \cd{block_inputs} 325--333.}
{$\mathcal{R}=0.95$\,A/W, $I_{FS}=40\uA$, LSB $=40\uA/65\,535=0.610$\,nA, so 1556 codes per \uW{} of drop power.

\subsection{Feedback for this plant}
Your loop works in codes. The plant chain is:
heater code $\to$ heater power ($\propto\mathrm{code}^2$, with TCR droop) $\to$ ring temperature (Foster poles)
$\to$ resonance shift ($s$) $\to$ detuning (divided by the self-heating relief) $\to$ monitor code (dip slope times
monitor gain). Every disturbance is slow compared with the loop, so you need integral action. \texttt{pi\_lock} uses
a PI controller in velocity form (it computes the change of the code, not the code). It puts the PI zero $T_i$ on the
dominant pole. It divides the error by the slope $s=dy/du$ that it measured at the set-point (here $s$ is in ADC
codes per heater code). The open loop is then about an integrator with a delay $\theta$ (not in the textbook):
\begin{gather*}
e_k=\frac{y_k-f\,M_{pk}}{|s|}\,\sigma(u_k),\qquad
\Delta u_k=k_c\Big[(e_k-e_{k-1})+\frac{T}{T_i}e_k\Big]+v_k,\qquad k_c=2\pi f_cT_i,\\
L(s)\approx\frac{\omega_c\,e^{-s\theta}}{s},\qquad \mathrm{PM}=90^\circ-\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}\,\omega_c\theta,\qquad
e_{ss}=\frac{\dot r}{\omega_c}\ \ (\text{ramp}).
\end{gather*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $f$ is the set-point fraction. $M_{pk}$ is the measured peak code.
  \item $\sigma(u)\approx u_{ref}/u$ is the gain schedule of the voltage law. $T$ is the controller period.
  \item $v_k$ is an optional type-2 term, a second integrator (zero by default). In $L(s)$, $s$ is the Laplace variable.
  \item The delay, not the thermal pole, limits the crossover. The delay is the sum of the window, the ADC latency,
  the wait for the next edge, the DAC latency and the hold (\cd{timing.py}).
\end{itemize}

\hard{The model plant has two main poles, a slow tail, a faster optical path, and a gain that changes with the code
and the world. Design on the integrator-plus-delay picture. Then check it on a measured step response. An open
question: a harness test holds the A1 peak rate within about 2\,pm (with a 20\,K swing, so the heater keeps its
authority). On the engine, the A3 test (a laser sweep at 50\,nm/s) leaves a 13.9\,pm lag. Find the cause before you claim a
bandwidth.}

\subsection{Scoring: lock error, the oracle and the OMA penalty}\label{sec:score}
The model knows the true detuning, so it scores the lock directly. The lock error is $e=d-d_{ref}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A ring first locks at $|e|\le\theta_{lock}$. It unlocks at $|e|>\theta_{lock}$. It relocks at
  $|e|\le\theta_{lock}-h_{yst}$ ($h_{yst}$ is the hysteresis).
  \item The default reference is the line ($d_{ref}=0$). A side-of-fringe lock sits 0.3--0.5 FWHM from its line by
  design. So the example files use \texttt{self\_reference}: $d_{ref}$ is the median detuning over the last 5\,ms of a
  20\,ms pre-run.
  \item At each instant the oracle finds the stable detuning with the best relOMA. The OMA penalty compares your
  relOMA with the oracle's (not in the textbook):
\end{itemize}
\[
d^{*}=\arg\max_{d\,:\,G_{sh}\,\partial P_{abs}/\partial\lambda_{res}<1}\ |T_1(d)-T_0(d)|,\qquad
\mathrm{penalty}=10\log_{10}\frac{\sum_t \mathrm{relOMA}^{*}}{\sum_t \mathrm{relOMA}}.
\]

\hard{The penalty is measured against the best stable point now, not against your set-point. If the conditions
change and your set-point does not move, you pay a penalty with zero lock error. Compare the traces \texttt{reloma}
and \texttt{oracle\_reloma} of one run to see it.}

\subsection{Uncertainty worlds}
Many device numbers are unknown. The preset stores ranges or competing hypotheses for them. A world is one draw of
all of them, shared by every ring of the die. Variation is separate: it spreads the geometry, heater and
responsivity of each ring inside a world. A run uses the nominal world, the 13 named corners, or many sampled worlds
(LHS). A claim holds only if it holds in every world $b$, for every ring $n$ and every time $t$ (not in the textbook):
\[
\text{claim}\iff\max_{b}\ \max_n\ \max_t\,|e_{b,n}(t)|\le5\ \mathrm{pm}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathrm{wrong\_line}_b=0\ \ \text{for all } b.
\]

\hard{No controller can hold some worlds. The heater supply limit binds in about 1/3 of the sampled worlds
(KNOWN\_ISSUES item 40). Ring 0 has no heater left at the hot end of A1 (code 0). Read the warm-start flags
(\texttt{no\_code}, \texttt{order\_hopped}, \texttt{supply\_limited}) in the world summary before you blame the
controller.}
