ECE496 capstone · evaluation report · draft for review

The ECE496 thermal locking controller on the G26 transmitter model

We took the thermal locking controller (TLC) that our team designed on a simple 4-ring plant, moved it unchanged into mrm-model, an independent, higher-fidelity simulator of an 8-ring microring transmitter, and measured how well it keeps every ring locked. This page summarises the method, the results so far, and the decisions we need.

Summary

The port itself is sound: the controller code running in mrm-model is bit-for-bit identical to the team's source and reproduces its published results. On the G26 plant the controller keeps rings locked through slow disturbances, but with errors far larger than mrm's own model-based controllers, and it loses lock under several fast or large disturbances. The cause is mostly a mismatch between the controller's built-in plant model and G26, not the port and not the dither waveform.

1Objective and scope

The TLC was designed and validated only on the team's own purpose-built 4-ring plant. The question is whether it still works on a plant it was not designed against. mrm-model provides that plant: a physics model of an 8-ring O-band transmitter (G26 preset) with its own thermal network, optics, monitor ADC and heater DAC.

  • In scope: run the controller as designed, on every disturbance both projects define, and report the numbers honestly, including failures.
  • Out of scope (for now): re-tuning the controller for G26. Any tuning will be a separate, disclosed second arm so that the faithful result stays clean.
  • Deliverable: one command (tools/tlc_run_all.sh, about 10 min) that runs the full scenario matrix and writes the report.

2System under test

The controller in brief

Each heater carries a small tone (the dither). Demodulating the monitor photodiode at the tone gives the slope of the ring's resonance (z1) and at twice the tone its curvature (z2). Their ratio ζ = z2/z1 does not depend on optical power, so an unscented Kalman filter (UKF) uses it, together with the photodiode's DC level, to estimate each ring's detuning. A model-predictive controller (MPC) then sets the heaters, and a supervisor handles start-up (ACQUIRE), tracking (TRACK), squelch and fallback to a PI loop.

3Method

3.1 Faithful port and fidelity gate

The controller is copied, not rewritten. The only edits are packaging (relative imports) and making the ring count a parameter instead of a hard-coded 4. Two automated checks guard this: the copied core must produce bit-identical outputs to the source on the source's own plant, for both sine and triangle dither, and it must reproduce the source's published steady-state (R1), aggressor (R3) and recovery (R4) results. Both pass, so any poor number on G26 belongs to the design, not to the port.

3.2 Controller parameters from the nominal design

The controller needs plant constants (heater gains and time constants, crosstalk, linewidth, monitor noise). We derive every one from the G26 nominal design model, never from the simulated world, exactly as mrm does for its own model-based controllers. Examples: heater paths split into a fast and a slow pole; crosstalk 3 % to the neighbour and 1.5 % beyond; monitor noise 3.9 × 10⁻⁵ of full scale.

3.3 Scenario matrix and comparators

Each ECE496 requirement (R1–R22) is mapped to an mrm scenario: steady state, die-temperature ramp, laser drift, local and common aggressors, a +3 dB laser power step, squelch (modulation off), a +10 K kick, a photodiode dropout, a forced fallback and cold start. Disturbances G26 does not model natively are added as labelled extensions. Every scenario runs with the TLC, the TLC's own PI baseline and mrm's four controllers (PI, dither lock-in, Kalman-LQG, MPC); cold start compares against mrm's WDM supervisor.

3.4 Metrics and pass bars

Error is each ring's detuning minus mrm's lock reference, in picometres (1 GHz ≈ 5.7 pm at 1310 nm). We grade it against two bars: mrm's 5 pm budget and the ECE496 2 GHz ≈ 11.4 pm threshold. We report rms and peak per ring, and a "tracking" error with the controller's own dither tones removed, so the dither is not counted as error.

3.5 Statistics and robustness

Rates such as wrong-line locking use exact 95 % Clopper–Pearson intervals. Robustness is tested on four alternative plants ("corners": fast, slow and textbook thermal, TEC-held package), once with controllers told the corner's model and once with the nominal model on the corner plant (a deliberate model mismatch).

4Results

4.1 Every scenario, every controller

In quiet or slowly changing conditions the TLC tracks at 2–3 pm rms, about 100 times the error of mrm's Kalman-LQG and MPC. Under the local aggressor, the power step and the PD-side aggressor it loses lock, but so do most mrm controllers in the last two: those are hard scenarios for everyone. The TLC's distinct failures are squelch, the +10 K kick and cold start.

4.5 Dither waveform: sine vs triangle

The team's 20 Nov update adds waveforms that are cheap to generate on an FPGA (square, 3-level, triangle); on the 4-ring plant the triangle beat sine (0.55 vs 0.91 GHz steady). These waveforms need tone frequencies of exactly 100 kHz ÷ an even number. No set of 8 such tones avoids interfering with itself, so on G26 the source's 4 tones (3.8–7.1 kHz) repeat every 4 rings; crosstalk 4 rings apart is about 10⁻⁴ and does not matter. The "sine · sampled tones" arm uses the same tones with a sine wave, separating the tone change from the waveform change.

5Why the TLC struggles on G26

  • A weak detuning signal. With ζ nearly flat, the UKF gets little information about where each ring sits, so the error wanders by several pm instead of settling (Figure 7).
  • A plant model that does not match G26. The controller assumes two heater time constants and crosstalk to the three nearest rings only. G26 also has a millisecond-scale package path and a flat 1.5 % crosstalk to every ring. This mismatch explains the failures under cold start, the aggressor and the power step, which no dither change fixes.
  • Start-up has no knowledge of the laser grid. ACQUIRE takes the first fringe it finds, which on an 8-line grid is often the wrong one (Figure 4).
  • Squelch is invisible. The controller detects squelch from a drop in data-pattern noise, but the G26 monitor averages the modulation away, so it drifts 34 pm.
  • Ring 0 runs out of heater under the local aggressor for every controller. This is a plant limit, which is why tables exclude it.

8Next steps

  • Demo: use the sine dither; present the triangle result as "does not transfer to 8 rings yet".
  • After review: re-run the corner matrix with the updated controller and refresh the written summary.
  • If approved: a disclosed second arm that re-fits the controller's model and dither amplitude to G26, then the 300-world cold-start campaign.
  • Upstream: merge mrm-model's main once its engine is fixed; ask for the ECE496 results to be regenerated.

Future direction: FPGA emulation

mrm-model already has a binary, tick-locked interface for external controllers (mrm/sim/bridge.py), fixed-point helpers (mrm/control/fxp.py) and test-vector export for HDL testbenches. A hardware version of the controller can therefore be tested against the simulator in lockstep: the simulator sends each monitor sample block, the FPGA returns heater codes. This proves the logic and fixed-point behaviour, not real-time timing, because the simulator runs about 15× slower than real time. A sensible order is a fixed-point Python model, then Verilator/cocotb against recorded vectors, then the FPGA over the bridge. The dither, lock-in and PI loop map naturally to hardware; the UKF and MPC are better kept on a soft processor at first.

B. Glossary

Detuning and lock reference

A ring modulates well only when its resonance sits at a fixed offset from its laser line. Heat shifts the resonance by tens of pm per kelvin; the heater pulls it back. The lock reference is the offset mrm considers ideal; our error is the distance from it.

Dither and lock-in detection

A small heater tone makes the photodiode signal wobble. Multiplying by the tone and averaging (lock-in) extracts the wobble's size and sign, which tells which side of the resonance the ring is on. Each ring needs its own tone, chosen so that no tone, harmonic or mixing product lands near another ring's tone.

UKF and MPC

The unscented Kalman filter fuses the lock-in ratio and the DC photodiode level with a thermal model to estimate each ring's detuning and drift. The model-predictive controller plans heater commands a few steps ahead using the same model.

Wrong-line lock

All 8 rings see all 8 laser lines. A ring that locks to a neighbour's line looks locked to the controller but carries the wrong channel.

Corners and priors

Corners are alternative plants. "Corner priors" tells the controller the alternative model; "nominal priors" keeps the design model on the alternative plant, which tests model mismatch.

Clopper–Pearson interval

An exact confidence interval for a rate measured from a small number of trials. 1 failure in 64 trials gives 0.04 %–8.4 %, which is why larger campaigns matter.
