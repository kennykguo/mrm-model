5 · Cold start: the TLC's biggest failure

Fraction of ring-trials ending on a foreign laser line, with exact 95 % Clopper–Pearson intervals. The TLC's ACQUIRE grabs the first fringe it sees (~6 ms) on G26's denser grid.

6 · Model trust: do the conclusions survive the corners?

Rings 1–7 peak error under the A1 on-die aggressor and in steady state, per plant corner. Toggle whether controllers know the corner (corner priors) or still assume the nominal design (nominal priors = model mismatch).

Takeaway: steady-state ranking holds everywhere; whether anyone survives A1 depends on the corner, and the verdicts don't change with mismatched priors.

7 · Why: the lock-in sensor on G26

Open-loop sweep of ring 0. The slope channel z1 is healthy (0.87× model, SNR≈50). The UKF's detuning measurement ζ = z2/z1 barely moves on G26's wider line (span ≈ 2× its own noise), so the estimator is almost blind.

8 · Nov 26: Michael's triangle dither on G26

ECE496 ae9556b (Nov 20) adds sampled dithers (square, 3-level, triangle) for an FPGA; on their 4-ring plant the triangle beats sine (0.55 vs 0.91 GHz steady). We re-vendored it (bit-identical to the source for sine and triangle) and ran every TLC scenario with four arms. Sampled tones must be 100 kS/s ÷ even N; no 8 distinct such tones avoid each other, so the source's 4 tones (3.8–7.1 kHz) repeat every 4 rings. sine · sampled tones isolates that plan change from the shape.

Verdict: the plan change costs little (sine ≈ sine · sampled); the triangle shape itself costs 1–5 pm where the TLC holds lock and loses A1 much harder. Cold start, the power step and A1 fail for every arm: the bottleneck is the TLC's model of G26, not the dither. One world per cell (cold-start campaign: 8 worlds).
