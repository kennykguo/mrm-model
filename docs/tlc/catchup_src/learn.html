Thermal locking: why it's needed

A microring only modulates when its resonance sits on its laser line. Resonance shifts ~tens of pm per kelvin, so die heat, aggressors and laser drift push it off. Each ring has a heater; the controller trims it to hold a fixed detuning (the lock reference). The metric everywhere here is that error, in pm (1 GHz ≈ 5.7 pm at 1310 nm).

Dither lock-in (B1–B3)

The TLC wiggles each heater at its own tone (4–13.5 kHz) and demodulates the monitor photodiode at f (z1, the slope of the lineshape) and 2f (z2, curvature). Tones must not collide with each other's harmonics or intermodulation products, which is why G26's 8 rings needed a new tone plan. Rev 1 broke this rule.

ζ = z2/z1 and the UKF (B4)

The ratio cancels optical power, so the unscented Kalman filter can use it as a power-independent measurement of detuning. On G26's wider line ζ moves only ~2× its noise across the working range (§7), so the estimator gets little information. That is the root of the TLC's weak tracking.

Supervisor modes: ACQUIRE → TRACK → fallback (B5)

Cold start sweeps the heater until a fringe appears, then tracks it. With 8 densely spaced lines, the first fringe is usually someone else's line: a wrong-line lock. mrm's wdm_supervisor knows the line grid and the ring-to-line assignment, so it avoids this. Fallback swaps the MPC for a PI loop.

Faithful port and the fidelity gate

We vendor the source core unchanged; only the adapter is ours. The gate replays the source plant through the vendored core and requires bit-identical outputs plus the published R1/R3/R4 numbers. Any G26 result is then the ECE496 design's, not a porting bug.

Multirate adapter

mrm steps the plant every 1 µs and calls controllers every 10 µs. The core runs every 100 µs on a block of 25 monitor samples (250 kS/s) and plans 10 heater codes ahead; the adapter buffers samples and plays the plan out.

Corners and knowledge priors

Corners are alternate plants (fast, slow or textbook thermal; TEC-held package). With corner priors every model-based controller is told the corner's model; with nominal priors they keep the design model on the corner plant (model mismatch). The A1 verdicts are identical under both.

Clopper–Pearson intervals

Exact binomial 95 % confidence intervals for rates like "wrong-line ring-trials". They stay honest for small counts: 1/64 gives [0.04 %, 8.4 %], and 0 events still give a finite upper bound. That is why the 300-world campaign matters.

Two pass/fail bars

ECE496 2 GHz (≈11.4 pm) is the source project's threshold; mrm 5 pm is the G26 budget. The TLC usually passes the first and fails the second, so always ask which bar a claim uses.
