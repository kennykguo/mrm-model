# ECE496 TLC on the G26 model: results (first pass)

Faithful port of the ECE496 thermal locking controller (TLC), run closed loop on mrm-model's 8-ring G26 transmitter
and compared with mrm's own controllers. Numbers come from `docs/tlc/results.md` / `.json`, regenerated by
`tools/tlc_run_all.sh` (branch `feature/tlc-port`, ~10 min). Method and decisions: `docs/TLC_PORT_SPEC_AND_PLAN.md`;
how the pieces fit: `docs/tlc/WALKTHROUGH.md`.

## 1. Bottom line
- The framework does what the project needs: one command runs the TLC, its own PI baseline and five mrm comparators
  through 16 experiments, an 8-trial cold-start campaign and four model-trust corners, and writes per-ring metrics in
  pm and GHz against both the mrm 5 pm budget and the ECE496 2 GHz threshold.
- The ported core is the ECE496 controller, not a re-implementation: bit-identical to the source on the source plant,
  and it reproduces the published R1, R3 and R4 numbers.
- On G26 the faithful TLC **holds lock in every slow or moderate disturbance** (steady state, die ramp, laser drift,
  common-mode aggressor, PD dropout) at 2-3.5 pm rms, inside the ECE496 2 GHz (11.4 pm) threshold but not mrm's
  5 pm budget on every ring. mrm's PI, Kalman-LQG and MPC controllers hold the same cases at 0.01-0.2 pm.
- It **loses lock** under the A1 on-die aggressor, squelch and the +10 K kick, and its cold-start acquisition lands
  on foreign lines. Each of these was predicted from the plant differences before the runs.

## 2. TLC on the 4-ring plant vs on G26 (steady state)
| | ECE496 4-ring plant (published) | G26, nominal world |
|---|---|---|
| peak error | 0.38-0.65 GHz (2.2-3.7 pm) | rings 1-7: ≤ 6.3 pm (1.1 GHz); ring 0: 9.7 pm |
| rms error | 0.21-0.38 GHz (1.2-2.2 pm) | 2.1 pm mean |
| within ECE496 2 GHz | 4 / 4 rings | 8 / 8 rings |
| within mrm 5 pm | (not defined there) | 6 / 8 rings |

Removing the TLC's own dither tones from the error changes the steady-state peak only from 9.7 to 7.1 pm: on G26 the
error is tracking error, not dither.

every held case (6-11 pm rms, 40 pm peak) and fails where the TLC fails.

## 5. Model trust (corners)
Plant and every controller's nominal knowledge follow the corner (as mrm treats all model-based controllers).

| Corner | `tlc` steady (rms / peak, rings 1-7) | `tlc` A1 | `pi_lock_c` A1 (rings 1-7) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nominal | 1.8 / 6.3 pm | lost | 0.63 / 15 pm |
| fast thermal | 1.9 / 6.5 pm | lost | 0.61 / 12 pm |
| slow thermal | 1.9 / 6.1 pm | lost | lost |
| textbook thermal | 2.6 / 16 pm | lost | lost |
| TEC-held package (crosstalk) | 1.9 / 6.8 pm | **holds** (2.4 / 8.2 pm) | 0.27 / 3.2 pm |

- **Holds in every corner:** the TLC's steady-state behaviour (2-3 pm rms), and the ranking "mrm's model-based
  controllers beat the TLC in steady state by two orders of magnitude".
- **Depends on the corner:** whether anyone survives A1. Under slow or textbook thermal dynamics even `pi_lock_c`
  loses rings; under the TEC-held package the TLC holds. A1 conclusions are therefore stated per corner.

## 7. Deviations from the source (all disclosed)
- Ring count as a parameter; injected parameters; 250 kS/s monitor without decimation (FIR 60 kHz); priors from the
  nominal design; an 8-tone plan (4.0-13.5 kHz) under the source's own collision rule.
- Warm start seeds the estimator with the core's setpoint (the source passed the true detuning).
- Timing alignment: blocks start at the first ADC sample's time; the heater DAC latency is added to the dither delay.
- **R13 ER floor dropped to 0 dB on G26** (2.8-3.0 dB reachable vs 3.5 dB required, so the source B6 had no
  setpoint). Pending owner confirmation.

## 8. Limits of this pass
- One nominal world per scenario except the 8-trial campaign; the planned 300-trial campaign is open.
- The common-mode aggressor and the +10 K kick are die-wide approximations; the kick is local in the source.
- Corners give the TLC the corner's own priors; a model-mismatch run (g26 priors on a corner plant) is open.
- O5 (online calibration learning) and restart-one-ring are not mapped. See `PLAN.md`, open items.
