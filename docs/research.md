# Evidence, tool choices, and model limits

Research checked on **2026-10-09**. All external links below are primary sources: publisher records, author/company publications, official project documentation, or official vendor documentation. Access date for every web source is 2026-10-09. A vendor's feature description establishes availability, not superior accuracy or experimental validation.

The capstone needs one configurable plant interface for comparing controllers under temperature changes, optical disturbances, fabrication variation, and hardware constraints. The defensible approach is a custom compact simulator whose coefficients can be replaced by measurements or offline physics extraction. An expensive solver with an invented geometry would still model an invented device. Public information supports a **Lightmatter-informed research model**, not a validated Lightmatter digital twin.

## What the supplied OFC paper establishes

The source is R. Anand et al., *16-Wavelength 800-Gbps Bidirectional Link over Single-Mode Fiber Using Microring Transceivers*, OFC 2026, M4B.3. The supplied three-page PDF was read in full and Figure 3 was visually inspected. The [publisher record](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?origin=search&uri=ofc-2026-M4B.3) confirms the work and conference. Local source: `/home/kennykguo/Downloads/16-Wavelength_800-Gbps_Bidirectional_Link_over_Single-Mode_Fiber_Using_Microring_Transceivers.pdf`.

| Quantity | Evidence and interpretation | Model consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture | Each end has eight Tx MRMs, eight receive channels using coupled-microring filters, and an interleaver; Fig. 1 and section 2 | Configurable array size; Tx monitor and receive filter are distinct components |
| Rate and format | 50 Gb/s NRZ per Tx wavelength; 400 Gb/s each direction, 800 Gb/s combined | NRZ envelope is the source-matched baseline; 56 Gb/s eye characterization is a different experiment |
| Channel plan | Two eight-channel 400 GHz grids around 1310 nm, offset by 200 GHz | Generate grids in frequency; do not apply the composite 200 GHz spacing to a single bank |
| Thermal sensitivity | Approximately 15 GHz per degree C; section 1 | Magnitude constraint, not a measured curve at every temperature |
| Tx stabilization sensor | Photocurrent at the MRM drop port; section 3.2 | Sensor peaks near resonance; a through-port notch has a different slope |
| Slow disturbance | Package TEC sweep 35 to 105 degrees C, 31 h, eight lanes below 1e-9 BER; Fig. 3(a) | Separate package boundary disturbance from local heating |
| Fast disturbance | Adjacent on-chip resistive aggressor, 808 degrees C/s, 25 to above 105 degrees C, stated 100 ms period; section 3.2 | Local heat injection is physically different from changing laser frequency |
| Fast-test observation | BER sampled every 2 s remained near 1e-11; monitor stable | Evidence for aggregate stability, not a time-resolved guarantee of zero brief errors |
| Optical path | 1 km SMF28, six connectors, approximately 3 dB chip-to-chip loss | Optional link context; these are not the MRM's intrinsic insertion loss |
| Laser accuracy | Absolute frequency accuracy/stability within approximately +/-20 GHz is required by the interleavers; section 4 | Laser drift needs its own input; ring stabilization cannot fix an interleaver mismatch |
| Energy | Approximately 3 W combined; 1.7 pJ/bit SiPh and approximately 2.1 pJ/bit SerDes; section 4 | Array-system energy does not identify per-ring heater power |

For a silicon redshift, `df_res/dT < 0` and `d(lambda_res)/dT > 0`. At 1310 nm the small-change conversion gives `|d(lambda_res)/dT| = lambda^2/c * 15e9 = 85.86 pm/K`. That conversion is derived; the signed convention and the reference temperature must be stated separately. Fifteen GHz/K is approximate, so 85.86 pm/K is not a four-digit experimental measurement.

The printed swing, ramp rate, and period do not uniquely specify the fast waveform. A symmetric 80 K triangular cycle at 808 K/s would take approximately 198 ms, not 100 ms. Do not silently reconstruct it as such. A synthetic ramp with the published maximum rate is a controller stress scenario; raw time records are needed to reconstruct the experiment.

## Later public Lightmatter evidence and generation boundaries

The official [April 2026 OFC presentation](https://lightmatter.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/OFC-2026-16-lambda-Bidi_PAM4-Nikhil-Kumar.pdf), linked from the [company resource page](https://lightmatter.co/resource/high-bandwidth-optical-links-nikhil-kumar-senior-director-photonics/), was checked. Slide 7 identifies an approximately 15 micrometre diameter and a depletion PN junction. Slide 9 shows a controlled 0 to 105 degrees C operating range. Slide 10 reports greater than 2000 degrees C/s robustness; the rendered graph has nonlinear heating/cooling, approximately 130 ms cycles, and a derivative trace reaching roughly +2200/-2300 degrees C/s. These graphical estimates are read-offs, not raw data. Its photonics energy figure differs from the proceedings paper; they must remain separate reported results.

The [October 2025 SMTA presentation](https://lightmatter.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-SMTAI-Womens-Leadership-Session-DFX.pdf), linked from its [resource page](https://lightmatter.co/resource/smta12025/), shows a 400 by 200 nm silicon waveguide illustration on slide 15 and 3 micrometre **waveguide** pitch on slide 16. It does not reveal the G26 coupler gaps, heater layout, doping profile, oxide stack, or ring-to-ring pitch. These drawings do not supply a process design kit.

The [OFC 2025 device paper](https://lightmatter.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/OFC-2025-submission.pdf) describes the earlier monolithic single-wavelength Tx/Rx test vehicle. Use it for an explicitly labeled G25 parameter hypothesis, not as proof that all parameters apply unchanged to the G26 link. Likewise, L20 product specifications, M1000 packaging results, and Guide laser specifications describe different systems. The local textbook already recognizes this distinction in `docs/SOURCES_AND_GENERATIONS.md`; preserve it.

The later [September 2026 high-radix photonic interconnect paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01821) is primarily an architecture/workload study. It does not supply a complete replacement MRM compact model or justify changing G26 control coefficients to shipping-product values. Recency alone does not make a source a measurement of the target plant.

Neither the supplied paper nor these public drawings disclose a complete thermal network, junction response map, DAC transfer function, monitor front end, firmware timing, optical absorption fraction, backscatter distribution, or manufacturing covariance. A requirement to tolerate 125 degrees C comes from the capstone/textbook project context; it is outside the 105 degrees C demonstrations above. Simulate it as extrapolation and report it that way.

## Open tools that are useful now

No single program solves every optical, electrical, thermal, and control problem. The following choices avoid paid solver access; their open-source licenses still apply. The compact model should remain usable without installing every optional extractor.

| Tool and primary source | Appropriate job | Main tradeoff and availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NumPy](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/) and [SciPy](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/) | Compact equations, stiff ODEs, exact linear state transitions, interpolation, parameter fitting | Excellent reproducibility and modest memory use; physical correctness comes from equations and calibration, not the library name |
| [Femwell](https://helgegehring.github.io/femwell/) | Vector waveguide modes, bent-mode studies, dispersion, static/transient thermal extraction | Particularly useful for shared optical/thermal geometry; requires meshing and convergence checks. Documentation warns it can lag code. PN depletion example is analytical, not complete TCAD |
| [Femwell transient heater example](https://helgegehring.github.io/femwell/photonics/examples/metal_heater_phase_shifter_transient.html) | Fit thermal modes from mesh-based temperature transients | Demonstrates a workflow, not the target device's geometry or validated coefficients |
| [scikit-fem](https://scikit-fem.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) | Assemble custom FEM heat, electrostatic, or eigenmode problems | Flexible small Python backend; boundary conditions, elements, material laws, and model reduction remain the user's responsibility |
| [Meep](https://meep.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) | Full-wave coupler scattering, reflection, bend loss and resonance reference calculations | Mature FDTD and resonant-mode analysis; 3D ring simulations can use substantial RAM. A 2D result is a surrogate. Requires spatial, duration, PML, domain, and material convergence checks |
| [DEVSIM](https://devsim.org/introduction.html) | Junction electrostatics/carrier transport, capacitance and electrical dynamics | Finite-volume PDE solver, Python interface, DC/AC/transient, Apache-2.0; requires actual doping/material/contact inputs and subsequent optical mode-overlap calculation |
| [SAX](https://gdsfactory.github.io/sax/) | Compose complex scattering matrices into WDM spectra; differentiable circuit fitting | JAX frequency-domain solver; does not supply semiconductor or thermal dynamics. Docs currently show 0.18.2, Python >=3.11, Apache-2.0, and required KLU backend; pin an actually tested release |
| [Simphony](https://simphonyphotonics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) and [project](https://github.com/BYUCamachoLab/simphony) | Photonic circuit definitions and reusable optical compact models | MIT, Python; useful independent circuit framework. Its model library does not become a calibrated Lightmatter library by being connected to the simulator |
| [Elmer FEM](https://docs.csc.fi/apps/elmer/) and [heat-equation manual](https://ftp.csc.fi/pub/files/index/elmer/doc/ElmerModelsManual.pdf) | Package/substrate heat diffusion with multiple materials and boundary conditions | General multiphysics alternative for larger thermal extraction; meshing/integration effort and memory rise with 3D problem size |
| [gdsfactory/gplugins](https://gdsfactory.github.io/gplugins/) | Geometry, layer stacks, solver interfaces and reusable extraction pipelines | Workflow infrastructure rather than independent truth. Check each backend's license; connecting a paid cloud tool through an open client does not make its solver free |
| [ngspice OSDI/OpenVAF](https://ngspice.sourceforge.io/osdi.html) | Heater driver, monitor amplifier, parasitic and analog compact-model simulation | ngspice supports compiled Verilog-A through OSDI since v39. Its official page deprecates ADMS; not every Verilog-A construct or proprietary model is portable |
| [Xyce](https://xyce.sandia.gov/) | SPICE-compatible electronics, larger circuit simulations | Open source, serial/parallel execution; photonic compact equations and parameter sets still need to be supplied. Its ADMS path is distinct from ngspice's recommended path |

Meep's [official release page](https://github.com/NanoComp/meep/releases) and Femwell's [source repository](https://github.com/helgegehring/femwell) are the availability checks. Documentation for a development branch can describe features absent from an installed release. Archive tool versions, input geometry/materials, convergence settings, and exported parameters with each extraction. Do not attach a generic 'SOTA' label to an unbenchmarked combination.

Useful newer GPU/differentiable alternatives exist, but changing solver to chase speed requires proving convergence and agreement for the actual component. For this machine, prioritize cross-sectional mode solving, small coupler subdomains, and reduced thermal models before whole-ring 3D FDTD. Limit concurrent jobs and thread counts; estimate array/grid storage before allocating it.

## Where the textbook is helpful, and where the plant must go further

The inspected local sources are `src/physics/index.ts`, Chapters 9, 11, 12 and 15, and `docs/SOURCES_AND_GENERATIONS.md` in `/home/kennykguo/interactive-textbooks/lightmatter-capstone`. No textbook files were changed.

1. **Keep provenance, do not import REF as one measured device.** Many entries explicitly say assumed or derived. In particular, 10/300 microsecond poles and near/next-near crosstalk fractions are not Lightmatter measurements. The approximately 4.7 K/mW resistance depends on mixing a G25 tuning estimate with the G26 sensitivity; it is a useful hypothesis, not an independently measured G26 resistance.
2. **The thermo-optic discrepancy is a model question, not proof of impossible hardware.** Chapter 9 correctly uses group index and explains its approximately 50 pm/K versus the paper's approximately 86 pm/K conversion. Its confinement weighting is not a rigorous bound on a vector effective-mode derivative. [Payne et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11897098/) derive the full-vector distinction from approximate material-weighted formulas. Our generic Femwell perturbation with explicitly chosen material derivatives gives `dn_eff/dT≈2.04e-4/K` despite `dn_Si/dT=1.86e-4/K`; it still does not reproduce the undisclosed actual device. Fit the measured signed sensitivity and investigate its temperature reference. Include expansion in phase extraction: `d(lambda)/dT = lambda/ng * (partial neff/partial T + neff/L * dL/dT)`.
3. **Use the proper add-drop round trip.** `ringAddDrop()` uses the correct feedback amplitude `a*t1*t2`. `fwhmNm()` instead uses `a*t` while claiming applicability to symmetric add-drop; for equal couplers the product is `a*t^2`. Its expression is a narrow-resonance approximation, not an exact width. Solve the actual spectrum or use a rate model with consistent linewidth conventions.
4. **Lorentzian widgets are local approximations.** They omit other resonance orders, wavelength-dependent loss/coupling, splitting and coherent reflections. Use the periodic complex ring transfer for acquisition/FSR studies and a calibrated cavity ODE for high-speed optical dynamics. A photon energy lifetime is `Q/omega`; field amplitude lifetime is twice that. Define which rate the code uses.
5. **An FSR smaller than 2.8 THz does not alone prove a bank is unusable.** Eight 400 GHz channels span 2.8 THz, but whether another resonance causes damage depends on its distance from each laser, width, coupling, loss and filtering. Check alias spectra against the full channel grid instead of treating span as a universally necessary FSR bound.
6. **Carrier-depletion calibration needs reference densities.** The helper's `signedPow(delta_holes, 0.8)` is a heuristic for negative changes. A nonlinear density law needs the difference between the final and reference absolute-density responses, with spatial optical overlap. Prefer measured `frequency(V,T)` and `loss(V,T)` maps or DEVSIM plus modes; do not claim the heuristic is depletion TCAD.
7. **Retain bit polarity.** `modulatorLevels()` sorts the two powers to make positive OMA. That is fine for plotting a static eye opening but can hide data inversion after crossing resonance. A waveform model must retain the voltage assigned to bit 0 and bit 1; report signed and absolute OMA deliberately.
8. **Thermal paths must reflect where heat enters.** `stepThermal()` exactly advances a frozen-input two-pole surrogate, but shares poles/spatial crosstalk structure and applies ambient as a separate offset. A coupled capacitance/conductance network can represent independent heater, ring, substrate and package states; local heating, package boundary motion and laser detuning excite different paths. Optical absorption and heater resistance can make the forcing nonlinear, requiring substeps or an implicit solve.
9. **A monitor is not signed detuning.** The drop response has zero slope at its peak and two sides with the same reading. Light-power variation can mimic detuning. Acquisition, branch selection, normalization/reference power, integration, sensor filtering, latency and quantization belong in the observable interface; the controller must not receive simulator-only temperature or detuning truth by default.
10. **A thermal controller trace is not a BER validation.** Static NRZ levels or Gaussian-noise estimates do not include a full SerDes, receiver, ISI, jitter, pattern effects and error counters. Report detuning, optical margins and model-based estimates distinctly from measured BER, and compare controllers using held-out traces and shared disturbance/noise realizations.

## Accuracy roadmap and calibration gates

The first gate is analytic/numerical correctness: passive optical power conservation, physically positive conductances/capacitances, stable zero-input decay, correct frequency/wavelength conversion, rail identity, and sample/hold/delay behavior. Passing these proves equations and implementation agree, not that the device is calibrated.

The next gate is parameter identification: measured through/drop spectra, open-loop heater steps, actual electrical power, monitor conversion/timing, and multiring cross-responses. Fit several temperatures, power levels, biases and devices. Preserve covariance or joint samples rather than independently sampling unrelated ranges. Evaluate residuals and confidence intervals; reserve separate devices/traces/conditions for validation.

The final gate is hardware transfer: the same controller sees actual ADC/DAC units and timestamps, operates within measured actuator limits, and reproduces acquisition, tracking and recovery on held-out hardware tests. Predictions outside the measured domain, especially to 125 degrees C or a new package, remain extrapolation. The prioritized measurement plan is in [lightmatter-data-request.md](lightmatter-data-request.md).
